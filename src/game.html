    What the physics models
    • Electric motor: full torque from a standstill, then power-limited (20 hp ≈ 15 kW), topping out around 35 mph.
    • Grip-first handling up to about 2.1 g; ask for more and the front washes wide and scrubs speed. Karts only brake the rear axle, so stamping on the brake while turning hard lets the rear go: lift and countersteer to catch it, or you spin.
    • Barriers: a light touch nudges you off and costs a little speed. Leaning on them costs a lot, so riding the wall is slower than braking.
    • Slipstream: tuck in 1–9 m behind another kart for less drag and about 1 mph over the governor.
    • The braking zones into the last two hairpins are bumpy: grip and braking come and go over the patches.
    • Engine sound: pick a V10, V8 or V6 turbo with automatic shifts, just for fun, or the real kart's electric drive. The kart itself has no gears.
    • Quick, light steering. Full lock eases off as speed rises, so holding a turn at speed stays predictable.
    • Strong brakes (about 1.2 g in a straight line). Lifting off gives mild regen, enough for quick kinks.
    • Hold brake at a standstill to reverse, up to about 12 mph. Press throttle to stop and go forward again.
    • Sessions start in the pit lane (13 mph limiter). Races begin with a formation lap: follow the field round and stop in your grid box. When you're done, drive back into the pit lane and stop to end the session.
    • Kart contact: bumpers shove and scrub speed and can push you wide; a hard side hit can unsettle the rear.
